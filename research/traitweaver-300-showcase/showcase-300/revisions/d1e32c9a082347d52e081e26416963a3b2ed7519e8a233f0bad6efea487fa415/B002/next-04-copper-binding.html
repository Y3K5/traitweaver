Intracellular copper binding

What was reported

Source-system observation

Promoter exchange changed yeast copper resistance, and chelator competition found Cu(I) bound to Crs5 was more labile than Cu(I) bound to Cup1. The two measurements support expression and protein properties as separable contributors.

Enhanced effectiveness of copper ion buffering by CUP1 metallothionein compared with CRS5 metallothionein in Saccharomyces cerevisiae (1996) · abstract

Direct Cup1-bound mass in the recipient was not resolved by total-cell copper. Medium depletion and recoverable metal require separate measurements.

Named-recipient result

HeLa cultures expressing yeast CUP1 contained more total intracellular copper than empty-vector controls under the two assessed copper exposures (P<0.01). Viability was higher and reactive oxygen readout lower in reported stress comparisons. Whole-cell copper quantification cannot assign all of that copper to Cup1.
