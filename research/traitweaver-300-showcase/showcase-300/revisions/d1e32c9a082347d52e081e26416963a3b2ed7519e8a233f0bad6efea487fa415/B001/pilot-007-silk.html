Spider-silk protein production

The mammalian study reported soluble recombinant proteins of 60–140 kDa and separate fiber measurements. An engineered E. coli study measured protein production and tensile properties after external spinning.

Source context. Araneus diadematus and Nephila clavipes (named in the native and related records)

Reported context. Cultured mammalian cells (lineages unqualified), Escherichia coli BL21(DE3), reported metabolically engineered host

What was reported

Source-system observation

A. diadematus has a fibroin gene family with gland-specific expression associated with different native silks.

Silk properties determined by gland-specific expression of a spider fibroin gene family (1996) · abstract

The native species and fibroin family were checked at abstract depth. This source does not independently map the Lazaris donor constructs.

Named-recipient result

Cultured mammalian cells expressed soluble recombinant spider silk proteins. The authors separately spun a fiber from a 60 kDa ADF-3 product.

Spider silk fibers spun from soluble recombinant silk produced in mammalian cells (2002) · abstract

Publisher article access was denied. First-page text and the abstract support product size and qualitative material comparisons. Exact donor-to-cell-line assignments remain unqualified in the Lazaris example. External processing generated fibers from a soluble recombinant protein.

Named-recipient result

An engineered E. coli host produced a recombinant protein based on N. clavipes dragline silk. The authors spun and tested fibers externally.

Native-sized recombinant spider silk protein produced in metabolically engineered Escherichia coli results in a strong fiber (2010) · selected fulltext

Recipient produced a recombinant protein based on a spider sequence. Fibers were made and measured in a separate processing step. Native property ranges are literature comparisons. Readout properties depend on protein variant and processing.

Questions still open

  • Mammalian cell line and exact donor-to-protein mappings remain unqualified at the reviewed access depth.
  • Soluble recombinant protein, externally spun fiber and autonomous spinning are different capabilities.
  • Similar performance on some material metrics does not establish native-equivalent fibers.
