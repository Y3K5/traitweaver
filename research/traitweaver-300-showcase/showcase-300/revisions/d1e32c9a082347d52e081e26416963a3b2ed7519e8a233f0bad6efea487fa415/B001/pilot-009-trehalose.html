Traitweaver · B001 / 09

Trehalose-associated abiotic stress tolerance

Selected Pusa Basmati-1 rice lines carrying bacterial TPSP accumulated about 3–10 times the trehalose of nontransgenic controls across conditions and showed advantages on specific controlled stress readouts.

Source context. Escherichia coli

Reported context. Oryza sativa indica cv. Pusa Basmati-1

Targeted primary source check · Qualified human review pending

What was reported

Named-recipient result

Selected rice lines expressing a bacterial OtsA/OtsB fusion showed higher trehalose and improved measurements in controlled salt, drought and chilling challenges.

Trehalose accumulation in rice plants confers high tolerance levels to different abiotic stresses (2002) · selected fulltext

The rice study found no obvious relationship between trehalose amount and stress tolerance among evaluated transgenic lines. Pathway effects or sugar signaling remain plausible. Salt, drought and chilling were separate controlled challenges. Field yield, robust dose-response and transfer to other recipients remain unverified in this record. Tobacco and potato studies show host-dependent accumulation and developmental tradeoffs. They did not test the same rice fusion design.

Source-system observation

E. coli lacking trehalose synthesis lost viability faster after cold exposure. Restoring otsA and otsB restored trehalose content and viability under the tested conditions.

Trehalose synthesis is induced upon exposure of Escherichia coli to cold and is essential for viability at low temperatures (2002) · abstract and indexed selected fulltext

This is a bacterial viability endpoint. A shared stress phenotype does not identify the mechanism in rice.

Related evidence

A distinct plant transfer study reported low trehalose in tobacco leaves and no detectable accumulation in potato tubers at baseline. Tobacco with accumulated trehalose showed growth and leaf-shape changes.

Inhibition of trehalase activity enhances trehalose accumulation in transgenic plants (1997) · abstract

The tested host tissues and expression context differ. This is context-limited evidence rather than a failure to reproduce the rice study.
